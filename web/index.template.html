    What the firmware is doing

    Detumble. At release the satellite is spinning. The controller ignores attitude and uses the gyro alone to brake the rotation through the reaction wheel.

    Point. A complementary filter fuses the gyro with the sun sensor into an attitude estimate. A PID controller with anti-windup and a slew-rate limit steers to the target.

    Protect itself. A real sensor is never perfectly still, so identical readings mean it froze, and a persistent mismatch with the gyro means it’s lying. Either one drops the spacecraft into SAFE mode, which stops trusting the sun sensor and only damps rotation until the ground clears the fault.

    Manage momentum. Every correction leaves spin stored in the reaction wheel. Kick the satellite a few times and the wheel nears its 6,000 rpm limit. Above 3,000 rpm the firmware switches on the magnetorquers, electromagnets that push against Earth’s magnetic field, and holds pointing while the wheel spins back down.

    All control math runs in the 5 KB WebAssembly module built from the C++ flight software, with Q16.16 fixed-point arithmetic, exactly as on the microcontroller. Built by Sai Anand.
